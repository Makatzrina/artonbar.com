How this site makes money

Affiliate links, featured listings, sponsored posts. None of them change a review. The details, in plain words.

The short version

ArtOnBar makes money three ways: affiliate links, featured listings and sponsored posts. None of them change a review. Here's how each works.

Affiliate links

Some links on this site go to Booking.com, GetYourGuide and occasionally other partners. If you click one and book a hotel, a boat trip or a cocktail class, I may earn a small commission. It costs you nothing extra — the price is the same as going direct. These links are marked with rel="sponsored" for search engines and usually say "Booking.com" or "GetYourGuide" next to them so you can see what they are.

I only link to things I'd point a friend at: hotels near the bars I'm recommending, and activities that genuinely pair with a night out. I never recommend a bar because it's near a hotel I can earn commission on.

Featured listings

Bars can pay for a Featured position on their town page. When they do, it says "Featured" on the listing. The written review is still mine, still honest, and the star rating isn't for sale. If a featured bar is bad, the review says it's bad. See the rates and the rules.

Sponsored posts

Occasionally a bar, event or brand pays for a post about a new opening or event. It's labelled "Sponsored" at the top of the page, in plain words.

Advertising

The site may show display adverts served by Google AdSense. Google uses cookies to show ads based on your browsing; you can opt out at Google Ads Settings. Ads are placed in clearly marked slots and never inside the text of a review.

What I don't do

  • No free drinks, meals or stays in exchange for coverage.
  • No "pay to remove a bad review".
  • No fake reviews, no scraped reviews, no AI-written reviews presented as visits.

Questions? Ask me directly.
