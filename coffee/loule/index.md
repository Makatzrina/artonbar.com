## How to do coffee in Loulé

Loulé is a market town, and the market is where the coffee is. **bean17** is a counter inside the Mercado Municipal, Wednesday to Saturday mornings, with Ilona roasting her own beans behind it: single-origin espresso, a pour-over if you ask, a flat white that would hold its own in Lisbon, and a Dutch apple pie that is the reason half the regulars come. It's cash only, it's tiny, and when the market closes at one, so does the coffee. Go on a Saturday when it opens at eight and the market is at its best.

**Mellow** is the afternoon answer: a small room with a vintage look and mismatched china on Rua Dom Paio Peres Correia, a few minutes' walk from the square, with coffee made with care, homemade cakes by the slice, and cocktails once the day turns. Tuesday to Saturday, until half past three. If you want to sit for an hour with a book, this is the one.

Everything else in Loulé serves the Portuguese standard: a bica from a machine that hasn't been cleaned since the Euro, milk boiled rather than steamed. **Café Calcinha** on the square is the exception that proves it: the 1929 art-deco room is worth a visit on its own terms, the coffee is fine, and it's open every day until eleven at night, which neither of the two above can say. On Sunday, when both bean17 and Mellow are closed, Calcinha is your coffee.

## Loulé coffee, the quick answers
